01 / AEROSPACE MANUFACTURING

Northrop Grumman
VTOL Drone Manufacturing.

Manufacturing team member supporting composite fabrication, inspection, assembly, and design-for-manufacturing work for a VTOL fixed-wing paramedic aircraft project in collaboration with Northrop Grumman.

MY ROLE

I was responsible for fabricating composite layups and molds for the fuselage and wing structures, supporting dimensional verification, conducting quality inspection, and helping improve payload-system manufacturability.

TOOLS / SKILLS

ASA Aero, carbon fiber, Kevlar, fiberglass, composite layups, vacuum bagging, mold finishing, defect identification, FAIR, and DFM.

PROCESS

The wing and fuselage forms were first produced using ASA Aero plastic. After printing and preparing the forms, I began the composite layups, layering carbon fiber and Kevlar for the wings, or fiberglass for the fuselage. The parts were covered with breather, enclosed in vacuum-bagging film, and sealed with gum tape before vacuum consolidation and curing. Finished parts were trimmed, inspected, and prepared for assembly.

RESULTS / FUTURE WORK

Both drones were fully operational and met the project's mission requirements: vertical takeoff and landing capability, stable flight, and successful delivery of a rescue-patient payload. The work demonstrated how composite fabrication, inspection, and assembly support a complete aerospace system. In the future, we could look into alternative layup processes to reduce residue in the molds and the sanding and finishing work required afterward.

02 / COMPOSITES PROJECT

Composite Pickleball Paddle

Material, process,
then iteration.

This project focuses on using composite materials to design and manufacture a pickleball paddle while considering weight, stiffness, durability, control, and manufacturing repeatability.

Four layers of carbon fiber, an EVA foam honeycomb core, and four additional layers of carbon fiber.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Create a pickleball paddle that could compete with commercially available paddles.

TOOLS / SKILLS

Carbon fiber, EVA foam, 3D printing, waterjet cutting, curing, and sanding.

PROCESS

I first measured the dimensions of a commercially available paddle and then CAD-modeled the handle and side components. After that, I cut out the general paddle shape from carbon-fiber sheets. I repeated this process to create two face sheets, each with four layers of carbon fiber. After curing the sheets until they were fully hardened, I cut them to shape using a waterjet and sandwiched the EVA foam between them. Finally, I attached the sides and handle to the composite paddle face and sanded the surface to preference.

RESULTS / FUTURE WORK

This project is still in its prototyping phase and very much a work in progress. This paddle was not intended to be the final product, but rather a proof of concept to see whether the design and material choices would be viable. In the future, I plan on refining the design, exploring different composite material options, and testing different sanding techniques. Now that I have been able to make a physical prototype, I am excited to see how much better I can make it in the future.

MY GOAL

Develop a detailed, non-operational display replica that demonstrates CAD design, fabrication, enclosure design, and motion-tracking concepts.

TOOLS / SKILLS

CURRENT PHASE

The concept is still in the prototyping stage. Current work is focused on finding practical ways to machine the parts while preserving the intended visual design and keeping costs reasonable. Once the structure is finalized, I can plan how to package electronics into the display build.

FUTURE WORK
